## Ingredients

- 1 kg fresh mussels, scrubbed and debearded
- 60 g fermented black beans (douchi), rinsed and roughly chopped
- 6 clove garlic, minced
- 30 g fresh ginger, minced
- 2 red chillies, thinly sliced
- 3 tbsp neutral oil
- 90 ml shaoxing wine
- 360 ml chicken or seafood stock
- 45 ml soy sauce
- 45 ml oyster sauce
- 1.5 tsp sugar
- 1.5 tsp sesame oil
- 3 tbsp cornstarch
- 60 ml cold water
- 4 green onions, sliced

## Steps

1. Heat the neutral oil in a wok or large deep skillet over high heat until shimmering.
2. Add the black beans, garlic, and ginger; stir-fry aggressively for 90 seconds until fragrant and starting to color.
3. Add the chillies and toss for 30 seconds.
4. Pour in the shaoxing wine and let it sizzle and reduce for 30 seconds.
5. Add the stock, soy sauce, oyster sauce, and sugar; bring to a boil, then drop to a strong simmer.
6. Stir the cornstarch and cold water into a slurry, then stir it into the simmering sauce.
7. Cook for 1–2 minutes until glossy and lightly thickened, still saucy and pourable, not gloppy.
8. Add the mussels, toss well to coat, and cover with a lid.
9. Cook over high heat, shaking the wok occasionally, until all the mussels open, about 4–5 minutes. Discard any that stay closed.
10. Off the heat, drizzle with the sesame oil and scatter the green onions over top.
11. Serve immediately over steamed rice with plenty of sauce spooned over.

## Notes

Use whole fermented black beans (douchi) rather than pre-made black bean sauce — the flavour is significantly more complex. Rinse them but don't wash away all the brine. The sauce volume here is roughly triple a standard recipe; adjust the cornstarch slightly if you want it thinner or thicker for rice. Don't overcook the mussels — they tighten fast once they open.
